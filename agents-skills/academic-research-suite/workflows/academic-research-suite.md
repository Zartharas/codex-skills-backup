# academic-research-suite

## When this workflow applies

Use for end-to-end scholarly research and publishing: research-question refinement, literature or systematic reviews, evidence synthesis, study planning, manuscript development, peer review, venue selection, submissions, rebuttals, proofs, and corrections. Trigger when the user is conducting or publishing research. Route copyediting-only work to academic-manuscript-copyedit. Never fabricate sources, methods, approvals, data, or findings.

## Web execution boundary

This Web edition provides analysis, planning, review, templates, and verification guidance. It does not imply that a scanner, cloud account, repository, endpoint, HSM, SIEM, SOAR, or other external system is connected. Begin read-only. Require explicit authorization, confirmed scope, available tools, and rollback conditions before any live action.

Route each request to one narrow workflow. Do not load the full vendored suite.

## Route by intent

Choose only the mode needed for the current research stage and avoid loading unrelated workflows.

## Research and citation workflow

1. Define the question, scope, inclusion criteria, date range, languages, and deliverable.
2. Design a reproducible search strategy. Record databases, query strings, filters, dates, and deduplication decisions.
3. Grade sources by directness, authority, methodological quality, recency, conflicts, and relevance—not journal prestige alone.
4. Build a claim–evidence matrix. Track citation, access level, study design, population/context, result, limitations, and which claim it supports.
5. Synthesize agreements, contradictions, boundary conditions, and gaps. Do not vote-count studies as a substitute for analysis.
6. Draft only claims supported by the matrix. Verify citations after drafting and again before submission.
7. Record limitations, excluded evidence, unresolved claims, and the search cutoff date.
For systematic reviews or meta-analysis, follow the appropriate current reporting guideline and preregister when applicable. Do not call a review systematic without a reproducible protocol, screening record, and inclusion/exclusion accounting.

## Journal-publishing workflow

For journal-publishing work, verify current journal scope, article type, limits, reporting checklists, fees, licensing, preprint, data or code, AI-use, and disclosure policies from official sources.
Never recommend a venue from impact factor alone. Consider audience, topical and methodological fit, editorial scope, indexing, review model, timelines when published, open-access terms, fees, preservation, research-integrity record, and suspicious/predatory signals. Label estimates and unavailable facts.
Do not facilitate simultaneous submission, paper mills, fabricated reviewers, citation coercion, hidden authorship, salami slicing, manipulated images/data, or deceptive AI-use declarations.

## Output contract

For staged work, report:
1. Current stage and selected mode.
2. Inputs used and access limitations.
3. Artifact produced or changed.
4. Verification performed and unresolved items.
5. Decision required from the researcher.
6. Next gate, including any recommended model change.
Default to the user's language. Preserve requested citation style, but distinguish citation formatting from source verification.

## Required output discipline

- Separate observed evidence, source-supported facts, inference, assumptions, and recommendations.
- State tool and data limitations explicitly.
- Preserve exact technical literals, measurements, citations, and user-approved constraints.
- Provide validation steps, unresolved issues, residual risk, and the next authorization gate.
- Never claim execution, access, containment, compliance, certification, or verification that was not observed.

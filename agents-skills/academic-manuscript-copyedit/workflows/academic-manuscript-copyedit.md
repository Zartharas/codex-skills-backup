# academic-manuscript-copyedit

## When this workflow applies

Use to copyedit or audit an academic or technical manuscript, journal article, dissertation-derived paper, book chapter, reviewer revision, or reference list for natural prose, factual fidelity, citation integrity, venue compliance, and regressions from third-party edits. Preserve honest AI disclosure and protected technical content. Do not evade detection, fabricate sources, or replace the author’s contribution.

## Web execution boundary

This Web edition provides analysis, planning, review, templates, and verification guidance. It does not imply that a scanner, cloud account, repository, endpoint, HSM, SIEM, SOAR, or other external system is connected. Begin read-only. Require explicit authorization, confirmed scope, available tools, and rollback conditions before any live action.

## Governing rule

Improve the manuscript as scholarship, not as a detector-evasion artifact. Preserve the author's intellectual contribution, evidence, terminology, disclosed use of AI, and responsibility for the final work. Never promise that prose is “undetectable,” human-authored, plagiarism-free, or acceptable to a venue without evidence.
Apply priorities in this order:
1. Factual and methodological accuracy
2. Source and citation integrity
3. Preservation of meaning and authorial position
4. Clarity and specificity
5. Natural scholarly rhythm
6. Surface polish

## Select the operating mode

Infer the mode from the request. Use more than one only when the task genuinely requires it.
- **Copyedit:** improve prose while preserving claims and evidence.
- **Third-party edit audit:** compare an original with a Grammarly, editor, or co-author revision and identify regressions.
- **Citation and reference audit:** reconcile in-text citations, references, identifiers, and source support.
- **Submission-readiness review:** check venue limits, required sections, disclosures, and consistency without claiming acceptance.
- **Targeted passage repair:** revise only the supplied section and avoid unrequested global changes.

## Establish the evidence hierarchy

Use this order when sources conflict:
1. The author's supplied manuscript, data, code output, tables, and explicit instructions
2. The target venue's current instructions and editor or reviewer feedback
3. Official standards, laws, policies, and primary sources
4. Peer-reviewed or otherwise authoritative scholarship
5. Clearly labeled inference
6. General model knowledge only when the point is stable and non-material
Do not silently “correct” a source-supported statement using general knowledge. Flag the conflict and explain what evidence would resolve it.

## Pass 1 — integrity

Check claims, tense, units, numbers, citations, terminology, scope, and protected content before changing style. Mark unsupported claims, internal contradictions, and statements that overreach the evidence.

## Pass 3 — evidence and references

Verify that each material claim is supported by the cited source, not merely that a citation appears nearby. Reconcile markers and reference entries. Do not invent missing metadata, quotations, page numbers, DOIs, findings, experiments, or sources.

## Output contract

Deliver the requested revised text as the primary output. When reviewing a full manuscript or third-party edit, also provide:
- **Critical regressions:** exact before/after wording and why it matters
- **Unresolved evidence questions:** facts that cannot be safely fixed from the supplied material
- **Citation issues:** missing, orphaned, inconsistent, retracted, or weakly supported references
- **Venue issues:** only those supported by current venue instructions
- **Validation status:** what was checked, what remained inaccessible, and any assumptions
For high-risk edits, show the original and revision. For routine copyedits, avoid bloating the response with every minor punctuation change unless the user asks for a redline.

## Safety and integrity boundaries

- Do not help conceal required disclosure of AI assistance.
- Do not rewrite text solely to beat a detector or misrepresent authorship.
- Do not fabricate references, quotations, identifiers, experiments, participants, results, approvals, or source verification.
- Do not upload confidential manuscripts or participant data to external services unless the user has authorized that exact action and the service is genuinely available.
- Treat unpublished manuscripts, peer-review files, participant information, and proprietary data as sensitive.
- Do not claim access to Crossref, Scopus, Web of Science, Turnitin, iThenticate, journal systems, or local files unless that access exists in the current session.

## Completion gate

Before finalizing, confirm:
- Protected content was preserved or every justified correction was disclosed.
- Claims do not exceed the evidence.
- Citation markers and references reconcile as far as the available material permits.
- AI-use disclosure remains accurate.
- The prose sounds like a competent author in the field, not an agent performing a generic “human” style.
- No detector score or stylistic heuristic was treated as proof of authorship.

## Required output discipline

- Separate observed evidence, source-supported facts, inference, assumptions, and recommendations.
- State tool and data limitations explicitly.
- Preserve exact technical literals, measurements, citations, and user-approved constraints.
- Provide validation steps, unresolved issues, residual risk, and the next authorization gate.
- Never claim execution, access, containment, compliance, certification, or verification that was not observed.
